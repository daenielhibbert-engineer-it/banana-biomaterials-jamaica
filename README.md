# banana-biomaterials-jamaica
Research and computational modelling of biodegradable materials developed from Jamaican banana and plantain agricultural waste.
 BananaLoop
 Developing Biodegradable Materials from Jamaican Banana and Plantain Agricultural Waste

 Project Overview

BananaLoop investigates the potential of Jamaican banana and plantain agricultural waste as a feedstock for biodegradable materials.

The project explores how biomass composition and processing conditions influence the physical and mechanical properties of biodegradable films. It combines experimental investigation with computational analysis to identify promising material formulations and processing conditions.

Research Question

How do biomass composition and processing conditions influence the mechanical performance, water resistance and biodegradation of biodegradable films developed from Jamaican banana and plantain waste?
 
 Objectives

1. Investigate the material potential of banana and plantain agricultural residues.
2. Examine how processing conditions and material composition influence film properties.
3. Develop a dataset from experimental measurements and relevant scientific literature.
4. Build computational models to analyse and predict material performance.
5. Identify promising formulations and processing conditions through optimisation.

 Methodology

The project will involve:

- Scientific literature review
- Biomass characterisation and material preparation
- Controlled experimental testing, subject to laboratory access
- Statistical analysis and computational modelling
- Formulation and process optimisation

 Motivation

Jamaica produces valuable agricultural resources, yet agricultural residues can remain underutilised. This project investigates how chemical engineering principles can help transform locally available biomass into higher-value materials.
